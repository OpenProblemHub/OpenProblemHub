# Decision Process

This document defines how decisions are made when disagreement or ambiguity arises
within the community.

The goal is not consensus.
The goal is **consistent protection of problem quality**.

---

## Scope

This process applies to decisions regarding:

- Acceptance or rejection of problem submissions
- Requests for revision
- Problem lifecycle state changes
- Disputes related to review outcomes

It does NOT apply to:

- Technical solution choices
- Implementation details
- Personal disagreements outside submission context

---

## Core Principle

When in doubt, **default to the stricter interpretation**.

Quality erosion is harder to reverse than missed opportunities.

---

## Decision Levels

### Level 1: Single Reviewer Decision

- A Reviewer may independently:
  - Accept
  - Request revision
  - Reject

If no objection is raised within a reasonable review window,
the decision stands.

This is the default and preferred path.

---

### Level 2: Reviewer Disagreement

If two or more Reviewers disagree on a decision:

1. Each Reviewer must explicitly reference checklist items
2. Arguments must focus on:
   - Problem clarity
   - Validation strength
   - Constraint realism
3. Preference, novelty, or enthusiasm are not valid arguments

Resolution rules:

- If disagreement persists, the **more conservative decision** prevails
- Conservative means:
  - Revise over Accept
  - Reject over Revise

No voting is conducted.

---

### Level 3: Escalation to Maintainers

Escalation is allowed only when:

- Review process was not followed
- Checklist items were ignored
- A clear procedural violation occurred

Maintainers may:

- Enforce correct process
- Request re-review
- Clarify governance rules

Maintainers do NOT:

- Override reviewer judgment on problem quality
- Re-evaluate the problem content itself

---

## Author Disputes

If an author disagrees with a decision:

- They may request clarification
- They may submit a revised version
- They may reference checklist items in their response

They may NOT:

- Argue based on intent or effort
- Appeal to popularity or external interest
- Escalate without procedural cause

Repeated argumentative behavior is grounds for rejection.

---

## Revision Handling

A revision request must:

- Clearly list blocking issues
- Reference specific checklist failures
- Indicate whether re-review is required

Revisions are evaluated independently.
Acceptance is never guaranteed.

---

## Deadlock Resolution

If no resolution is reached after reasonable discussion:

- The submission is closed
- The problem may be resubmitted in the future
- No further justification is required

Closure is a valid and final outcome.

---

## Documentation Requirement

All final decisions must be documented with:

- Decision outcome
- Referenced checklist items
- Reviewer(s) involved

Transparency protects both contributors and reviewers.

---

## Final Principle

This community does not optimize for speed, growth, or harmony.

It optimizes for:

- Long-term signal integrity
- Consistent judgment
- Respect for real-world constraints

A decision that protects quality is always correct,
even if it is unpopular.
